# Kelvar Industries Website Render Brief

## Purpose

Kelvar Industries requires a cohesive set of 3D renders and motion assets for its website. The imagery should communicate credible engineering, physical scale, real-world operation, and the relationship between each platform and the wider Kelvar intelligence system.

The visual direction should feel like serious industrial engineering: physically plausible lighting, restrained camera movement, realistic environments, and clear product detail. Avoid heavily stylised science-fiction presentation, decorative HUD graphics, excessive lens flares, and effects that obscure the product.

## Priority Deliverables

For each sufficiently developed platform, the immediate priority is:

1. One cinematic environmental hero render
2. One clean three-quarter product render
3. One operational or deployment scene
4. Three technical-detail renders

This initial set will provide enough material to significantly improve the website while the complete render package is being developed.

## Complete Render Set

### 1. Environmental Hero Render

A cinematic, wide composition showing the platform performing its intended function.

- Use a landscape composition.
- Keep the platform clearly identifiable.
- Leave sufficient negative space for website headings and calls to action.
- Use realistic lighting, weather, scale, and atmospheric conditions.
- Do not bake text or interface elements into the image.
- Provide left-weighted and right-weighted compositions where possible.
- Supply a minimum master resolution of `3840 × 2160`.

Example scenarios include:

- StratoKite operating above cloud level
- Dome patrolling a residential or industrial perimeter
- A maritime platform operating around offshore infrastructure
- Robobot navigating a realistic ground environment

### 2. Clean Product Render

An isolated view that clearly communicates the platform's industrial design.

- Provide front and rear three-quarter views.
- Keep the complete product visible within the frame.
- Use a neutral dark-grey or transparent background.
- Use soft studio lighting without strong colour casts.
- Maintain a consistent camera height and focal length across platforms.

These renders will be used for platform cards, product introductions, and overview sections.

### 3. Orthographic Views

Engineering-style views without perspective distortion.

- Top
- Side
- Front
- Rear, where materially different
- Consistent scale and framing
- Transparent background preferred

### 4. Technical-Detail Renders

Close-ups of features that genuinely differentiate the platform.

Relevant subjects may include:

- Sensor arrays
- Camera or optical assemblies
- Propulsion systems
- Solar surfaces
- Balloon attachments
- Communications equipment
- Charging or docking mechanisms
- Landing structures
- Access panels
- Material and assembly transitions

Every close-up should support a technical story that the website can explain. Avoid decorative close-ups that do not communicate a real feature.

### 5. Scale Render

A composition that makes the platform's physical dimensions understandable.

Use one or more of the following:

- A human silhouette
- A vehicle
- A building
- Recognisable infrastructure
- A technically accurate dimension diagram

This is particularly important for StratoKite because its scale is difficult to infer from isolated renders.

### 6. Deployment and Operating Sequence

Provide three or four images showing the system's operating lifecycle:

1. Transport or storage
2. Setup or launch
3. Active operation
4. Recovery, landing, or docking

The sequence must reflect the platform's intended operation accurately. These images will be presented as a meaningful process rather than as decorative numbered cards.

### 7. System Architecture Render

A diagrammatic composition showing how the platform interacts with the wider Kelvar system.

The composition may include:

- The platform
- Sensors and payloads
- KAIN or AI Command
- Communications links
- A ground station
- Other connected platforms

Where possible, provide the clean visual without baked-in labels. Website labels and explanations should be implemented as responsive HTML so they remain legible and accessible.

### 8. Exploded or Cutaway View

Where the product design is sufficiently resolved, provide a technically accurate exploded or cutaway render.

- Separate major assemblies clearly.
- Expose relevant internal components.
- Preserve understandable mechanical relationships.
- Do not invent components merely to make the product appear more sophisticated.

## StratoKite-Specific Requirements

Please provide the following for StratoKite:

- A wide atmospheric hero with substantial negative space
- A clean three-quarter studio render
- Accurate top, side, front, and rear profiles
- A balloon deployment or inflation scene
- A balloon-assisted ascent scene
- A transition from ascent into aircraft operation
- A solar-wing close-up
- A propulsion and rotor close-up
- A sensor-payload close-up
- A communications or data-link visualisation
- An accurate human-scale comparison
- A system view showing StratoKite feeding information into KAIN
- A short animation covering launch, ascent, and flight

The balloon, attachment, aircraft body, wings, solar surfaces, rotors, sensors, and tail assembly must remain consistent across every view. Inconsistent geometry between renders can make the platform appear speculative rather than engineered.

## Motion Deliverables

Where animation is available, provide short modular sequences rather than a single long promotional film.

Recommended sequences include:

- Slow product rotation
- Launch or deployment
- Propellers beginning to rotate
- Balloon-assisted ascent
- Transition into forward flight
- Sensor scanning
- Formation or connected-system operation

### Motion Direction

- Keep each sequence approximately 6–10 seconds long.
- Create seamless loops where appropriate.
- Use a static camera or very slow, deliberate camera movement.
- Keep the product clearly observable throughout the shot.
- Avoid rapid cuts, aggressive orbiting cameras, excessive lens flares, and science-fiction HUD effects.
- Do not bake text into the video.
- Do not include sound unless specifically requested.
- Ensure the first frame can function as a website poster image.

## Technical Delivery Requirements

Please supply:

- `3840 × 2160` landscape masters
- Selected portrait compositions at approximately `2160 × 2700`
- Transparent PNG versions of isolated product renders
- High-quality animation masters in ProRes or a high-bitrate MP4 format
- Still poster frames for every animation
- `sRGB` colour-managed exports
- A contact sheet identifying every delivered asset
- Consistent, descriptive filenames
- Additional safe space around aircraft, wings, rotors, balloons, and other extremities for responsive cropping

Website-ready WebP and WebM derivatives will be created separately from the supplied master files.

## Brand and Accuracy Requirements

- All visible branding must read **KELVAR**, never **KEVLAR**.
- Do not bake headings, annotations, specifications, or interface graphics into final website images.
- Maintain identical product geometry, branding placement, materials, proportions, and component positions across every angle.
- Use physically plausible environments, lighting, shadows, reflections, weather, and atmospheric effects.
- Do not present unfinished or hypothetical functionality as an established product feature.
- Flag any render that includes an approximate or unresolved engineering detail.

## File-Naming Convention

Use descriptive, predictable filenames:

```text
{platform}-{scene-or-view}-{variation}.{extension}
```

Examples:

```text
stratokite-atmospheric-hero-left.png
stratokite-three-quarter-front-transparent.png
stratokite-orthographic-top.png
stratokite-solar-wing-detail.png
stratokite-balloon-assisted-ascent.mov
dome-industrial-perimeter-operation.png
```

Avoid random export names, versionless filenames, and generic names such as `render-1`, `final-final`, or `image-new`.

## Final Delivery Checklist

Before delivery, confirm that:

- [ ] All branding is spelled correctly.
- [ ] Product geometry is consistent across every view.
- [ ] Each platform has a hero, clean product view, operational scene, and technical details.
- [ ] Landscape renders include usable negative space.
- [ ] Product extremities are not cropped from the master files.
- [ ] Orthographic views use consistent scale and framing.
- [ ] Transparent-background versions are included where requested.
- [ ] No website text or UI labels are baked into the assets.
- [ ] Motion sequences have stable poster frames.
- [ ] Master files and website-preview exports are clearly separated.
- [ ] A contact sheet and descriptive file list are included.
